FIELD MANUAL / 002

STUDY THE SEQUENCE

These are learning outlines, not instructions to practise throws without a coach. Exact grips and entries can vary by side and teaching method.

01

KOSHI-WAZA / HIP

O-GOSHI

VIEW ANIMATION ↗
  1. Kuzushi · Off-balance

    With a coach-approved lapel/collar and sleeve grip, guide the partner's balance forward. Do not yank the neck or arm.

  2. Tsukuri · Entry

    Turn in close and position the hips in front of the partner's centre, keeping your own posture and balance. The coach should check your distance and foot placement.

  3. Kake · Execution

    The hip acts as a turning point as the partner is guided over. The instructor teaches the correct direction, control, and safe landing before any full throw.

  4. Review

    Can you explain why moving the centre of mass forward makes the entry more effective? Practise the entry slowly with supervision.

02

ASHI-WAZA / FOOT-LEG

O-SOTO-GARI

VIEW ANIMATION ↗
  1. Kuzushi · Off-balance

    Learn to guide the partner's balance backward under the coach's direction, while staying upright and stable yourself.

  2. Tsukuri · Entry

    Step into the correct position beside the partner. Keep the chest and posture controlled; do not collide or rush the entry.

  3. Kake · Execution

    The reaping leg and upper-body direction work together to remove support. Because this can cause a hard fall, only a coach should teach and supervise the actual action.

  4. Review

    Why does timing matter when the partner's weight is on the target leg? Discuss it with your instructor before practising.

03

TE-WAZA / HAND

IPPON-SEOI-NAGE

VIEW ANIMATION ↗
  1. Kuzushi · Off-balance

    Guide the partner's balance forward through a supervised grip and coordinated movement, not by pulling the arm forcefully.

  2. Tsukuri · Entry

    Turn in and lower your level with bent knees, keeping your back and head in a safe, coached position. Alignment is especially important.

  3. Kake · Execution

    The partner is guided over the shoulder line. This requires accurate positioning and ukemi; do not attempt a full throw from written instructions alone.

  4. Review

    Compare this shoulder-based leverage with O-goshi's hip-based leverage. Which body position acts as the turning point in each?

!

SAFETY FIRST: Learn ukemi (breakfalls) before throwing practice. Use a qualified instructor, an appropriate mat, and a willing partner. Never practise these throws on friends or family as a game.
