FIELD MANUAL / KUZUSHI STUDIES

BREAK THE BALANCE.

Before studying the full throw, understand where the partner's balance needs to go. Each technique has a characteristic direction of kuzushi. The arrows below describe the general direction of uke's imbalance, not necessarily the direction tori moves.

FIRST, UNDERSTAND THE DIRECTION

FRONT, BACK, OR DIAGONAL?

Imagine the partner standing upright. Their front is the direction their toes and chest face; their back is the direction of their heels. A forward direction is toward the front of their body. A backward direction is toward the rear. Many techniques use a diagonal direction rather than a perfectly straight line. The direction can be mirrored when the technique is done on the opposite side.

KOSHI-WAZA / HIP TECHNIQUE

O-GOSHI

大腰 · Major hip throw

HOW TO READ THIS TECHNIQUE

FORWARD KUZUSHI

O-goshi is a hip technique. Its kuzushi generally guides uke's balance forward before the hip-based turning movement. The aim is to disturb the relationship between the centre of mass and the base of support, rather than simply pull the partner upward.

While watching the video, look at the movement before the throw becomes obvious. Pay attention to how the upper body, the partner's feet, and the direction of travel relate to one another.

MAIN IDEA

The characteristic direction is forward, often toward a forward diagonal. The direction is based on uke's balance, and the exact angle depends on the side, grip, and teaching variation.

PHASE 01 / THE MAIN FOCUS

WHERE DOES THE BALANCE GO?

For O-goshi, study the forward direction of imbalance. A forward movement is toward the front of uke's body, where the toes point. Depending on the entry and side, the line may be forward and slightly diagonal.

FORWARD
↑
TOWARD THE TOES

Forward, sometimes forward-diagonal

The general aim of kuzushi is to guide uke's balance toward the front of their base of support. In the demonstration, observe how the practitioner's controlled upper-body movement relates to the direction uke's body begins to travel.

Look for: the transition from a balanced posture to a forward shift. The direction is not the same as lifting someone straight up or pulling them randomly toward yourself.

Do not confuse direction with force. Kuzushi is not always a literal push. Depending on the grip and movement, a practitioner may use controlled pulling, pushing, turning, or a combination. The key observation is the direction in which uke's balance is being guided.

THE THREE PHASES

Use these phases to organise what you observe in the video.

01

KUZUSHI · 崩し

The balance-breaking phase. Study how uke's balance is guided forward and whether the movement places their centre of mass closer to the front boundary of their base of support.

OBSERVE Which way is uke's body beginning to move? Does it match the forward direction associated with O-goshi?
02

TSUKURI · 作り

The fitting-in and positioning phase. Observe how tori's position changes in relation to uke. This is where body position and the intended hip-based turning relationship become easier to see.

OBSERVE How does the entry relate to the earlier direction of imbalance?
03

KAKE · 掛け

The execution phase. The movement continues into the throw. Observe the direction of rotation and how the demonstration maintains control through the finish.

OBSERVE Can you recognise the earlier forward imbalance in the final movement?

WHAT TO LOOK FOR

  • Forward movement before the main turning action.
  • The relationship between uke's body and feet.
  • How the entry relates to the forward direction.
  • How the full movement connects rather than appearing as isolated actions.

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

  • Thinking kuzushi means pulling upward.
  • Assuming forward always means perfectly straight ahead.
  • Ignoring how the angle can change with the side and grip.
  • Thinking a correct direction alone is enough to perform a throw safely.

O-SOTO-GARI

大外刈 · Major outer reap

HOW TO READ THIS TECHNIQUE

BACKWARD KUZUSHI

O-soto-gari is a foot-leg technique. Its general kuzushi direction is backward, often toward a rear diagonal. The relationship between uke's balance, supporting leg, and upper-body movement is important to observe.

Do not focus only on the reaping leg in the video. Watch what happens to the upper body and balance before the reaping action. This helps explain why timing and direction work together.

MAIN IDEA

The characteristic direction is backward, often toward a rear corner. Observe how the change in balance relates to the supporting leg, rather than treating the technique as a leg swing by itself.

PHASE 01 / THE MAIN FOCUS

WHERE DOES THE BALANCE GO?

For O-soto-gari, study the backward direction of imbalance. Backward means toward the rear of uke's body, where the heels point. The line often has a diagonal component, depending on the side and situation shown.

BACKWARD
↓
TOWARD THE HEELS

Backward, sometimes rear-diagonal

The general aim of kuzushi is to guide uke's balance toward the rear of their base of support. As you watch, notice the change in posture and how the upper-body movement relates to the leg action.

Look for: the backward shift that comes before and accompanies the main action. Pay attention to which leg supports uke and how balance changes around that support.

Backward does not mean pulling straight down. The direction describes where uke's balance is being guided, not a single hand movement. The precise angle can vary with the entry and side. The actual technique and any leg action must be learned under direct coaching.

THE THREE PHASES

Follow the balance change before focusing on the leg action.

01

KUZUSHI · 崩し

The balance-breaking phase. Focus on the general backward direction and the way uke's posture changes. This is more informative than looking only at the leg action later in the throw.

OBSERVE Does uke's balance begin to shift toward the rear before the main leg action?
02

TSUKURI · 作り

The positioning phase. Observe where tori is positioned relative to uke and how this relates to the supporting leg and the intended direction of imbalance.

OBSERVE How does body position relate to the direction uke's balance has been guided?
03

KAKE · 掛け

The execution phase. Observe how the upper-body movement and leg action are coordinated. A leg movement alone does not explain the full technique.

OBSERVE How does the reaping action relate to the earlier backward shift in balance?

WHAT TO LOOK FOR

  • The general backward or rear-diagonal direction.
  • How uke's posture changes before the leg action.
  • The relationship between balance and the supporting leg.
  • How upper-body direction and leg action work together.

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

  • Assuming the leg action alone creates the throw.
  • Confusing backward imbalance with simply stepping backward.
  • Ignoring the diagonal direction that may be used.
  • Trying to test the reaping action on another person without instruction.

TE-WAZA / HAND TECHNIQUE

IPPON-SEOI-NAGE

一本背負投 · One-arm shoulder throw

STUDY 003
THROW DEMONSTRATION VIDEO REFERENCE
OPEN VIDEO ON YOUTUBE ↗

HOW TO READ THIS TECHNIQUE

FORWARD KUZUSHI

Ippon-seoi-nage is a shoulder throw. Like O-goshi, its general kuzushi direction is forward, often with a diagonal component. However, the positioning and turning relationship differs from a hip throw.

Pay attention to the change in uke's balance before the main turning movement. Then observe how tori's position changes relative to uke and the intended direction of movement.

MAIN IDEA

The characteristic direction is forward, often forward-diagonal. Compare this with O-goshi: both generally use forward kuzushi, but they differ in their entry, body position, and turning relationship.

PHASE 01 / THE MAIN FOCUS

WHERE DOES THE BALANCE GO?

For Ippon-seoi-nage, study forward imbalance, commonly with a diagonal component. Front means toward uke's toes. The exact angle depends on the side and variation shown in the demonstration.

FORWARD
↗
FRONT / DIAGONAL

Forward, often forward-diagonal

The general aim is to guide uke's balance forward before the turning action. Watch for the relationship between uke's upper-body movement and the direction the body begins to travel.

Look for: a forward shift followed by the turning movement. Compare the general balance direction to O-goshi, then look for the differences in how tori positions the body.

Same general direction, different technique. O-goshi and Ippon-seoi-nage can both use forward kuzushi. This does not make them the same throw. The entry, body position, and turning relationship differ. The exact angle is not identical in every variation.

THE THREE PHASES

Compare the forward imbalance with the shoulder-based entry.

01

KUZUSHI · 崩し

The balance-breaking phase. Observe the general forward direction and how uke's balance changes before the main turning movement.

OBSERVE Is uke's balance being guided forward, and does the movement include a diagonal component?
02

TSUKURI · 作り

The positioning phase. Observe how tori turns into the shoulder-based position and how that entry relates to the earlier forward imbalance.

OBSERVE How is the turning entry different from the hip-based position seen in O-goshi?
03

KAKE · 掛け

The execution phase. Observe how the turning movement and upper-body control lead into the throw. Pay attention to control through the finish.

OBSERVE How does the final movement relate to the earlier forward direction of imbalance?

WHAT TO LOOK FOR

  • Forward imbalance before the turn.
  • Any diagonal component in the direction of movement.
  • How the entry differs from O-goshi.
  • How the practitioner maintains control throughout the demonstration.

COMMON MISUNDERSTANDINGS

  • Assuming all forward-direction throws use the same entry.
  • Watching only the shoulder area and ignoring uke's balance.
  • Assuming the arrow must be perfectly straight forward.
  • Attempting a shoulder throw without supervised practice and breakfall training.

SAFETY FIRST

These direction notes are for understanding and video observation, not for practising a throw from written instructions alone. Learn with a qualified judo instructor, on suitable mats, and after learning how to fall safely. Do not test throws on friends or family.
